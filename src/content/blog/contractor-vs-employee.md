## Introduction

Many people are considering freelance or contract work in the gig economy, but they may not have a clear understanding of the differences between it and traditional employment.

I have worked for several years as a permanent full time employee, and then I worked for many years as a contractor, and I experienced both situations and touched the pros and cons of each, and I think that I can describe clearly and from my personal experience, the benefits and the challenges of each.

In this article, I will discuss the main differences between working as an independent contractor and working as a permanent employee, as well as the advantages and disadvantages of each based on my personal experience.

## Income

Due to the risky nature of working as a contractor, the income of working as a contractor in some cases can be higher than that of working as an employee. Also most of the benefits and perks that are usually included in an employee’s package are not given to contractor, so the high salary can compensate.

## Handling logistics

When you travel for work as a permanent employee, your company books your flight tickets, arranges your accommodation, your transportation, gives you a SIM card and a laptop, and provides you with health insurance.

However, as an independent consultant, you need to handle all your logistics, especially if your contact is based on an all-inclusive rate or on a project-by-project basis. But fortunately, there are many tools that a contractor can use to handle these logistics effectively such as online tools and this also means that you have more control over your working conditions and can tailor your arrangements to your specific needs and preferences.

## Experience diversity

As a contractor, you have the opportunity to work in many roles, in different countries, and for different companies which broadens your experience and skills, In contrast, employees may not have the opportunity to work on diverse projects and for multiple clients and companies, which may limit their experience.

## Stability

As a contractor, you do not enjoy the same level of job security as a permanent employee, your contract is dependent on project continuity and in many cases there may be a case of uncertainty, so your contract may be extended for short-term extensions, such as three months, which limits your ability to plan for the long term.

It also worth mentioning here that being a permanent employee does not ensure full job security as employees are also vulnerable to job loss due to layoffs, downsizing, or restructuring.

## Freedom and flexibility

One of the significant benefits of being a consultant or contractor is the freedom to choose the projects and clients to work with, and the role to play in these projects, and the flexibility to move from a project to project and being able to take long vacations between contracts.

## Career Path

As a contractor, you have to handle your career growth and to prove your competence for a better role, which may be more challenging than being a permanent employee with a traditional preconfigured career path, However, you can create opportunities for career growth by carefully selecting the roles and projects that can help you achieve your career growth goals.

## Retirement

As a consultant or contractor, you are responsible for your own retirement savings plan while permanent employees have this plan set for them by their employer already, but this gives you more flexibility and control over your retirement savings and investment strategies.

## Conclusion and How to choose the better for you?

If independence and flexibility are high priorities for you, and you prefer to decide each and every aspect in your career and can take risk, then becoming a consultant or contractor may provide a fulfilling experience, on the other hand, if you prefer stability and job security is of utmost importance to you, then being a permanent employee is the right choice for you and it’s important to consider personal preferences, financial goals, and career aspirations before making a decision.
